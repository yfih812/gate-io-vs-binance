# gate io vs binance: how to pick one for altcoin access, spot and futures fees, VIP tiers and liquidity

Search "gate io vs binance" and you get a wall of feature lists that end with "it depends." They're not wrong, but they skip the part that actually decides it: what you trade.

Binance is the biggest venue in the industry, and its order books on BTC, ETH and the large caps reflect that scale. Gate spent a decade as Gate.io and moved its primary domain to Gate.com in 2025, building its reputation on the opposite instinct — listing early, and listing a lot. Same industry, two different products.

Almost everything else in this comparison flows from that difference.

One shared constraint worth knowing before you deposit anything: neither platform serves US residents on its global version. Availability in other countries varies, so check the terms for your own region first.

## The short answer, if you don't want the long one

- **Deep books, large-cap pairs, biggest user base:** Binance. Its standard spot fee is 0.1% maker and 0.1% taker, cut by 25% when you pay fees with BNB — Binance itself quotes that combination.
- **Small caps, new listings, a fee ladder you can actually reach:** Gate. Its VIP tiers start at much lower volume thresholds, and the top of the ladder reaches 0% maker.
- **Neither, exclusively:** plenty of active traders run both, using Gate to discover and Binance to fill large orders.

If you already know which of those two habits sounds like you, that's the decision. The rest of this article is the reasoning, the numbers, and the caveats.

## What a spot trade actually costs

Gate's own fee overview lists VIP 0 spot trading at **0.10% maker / 0.10% taker**. Pay fees in GT, the platform's token, and that drops to 0.09% at the entry tier. Binance starts at the same 0.10% / 0.10%, but BNB fee payment takes 25% off, landing at 0.075%. Binance also runs periodic zero-fee promotions on specific pairs, usually tied to new listings or stablecoin pairs, with eligibility that often excludes regular-tier users.

So at the base tier, a casual spot trader pays less on Binance. That gap narrows fast, though, because the two ladders are shaped very differently.

### The 0.2% figure you keep seeing

A lot of articles quote Gate's spot fee at 0.20%, sometimes 0.15%. Gate's own fee page and help centre both list 0.10% / 0.10% at VIP 0, and that's the number that appears in Gate's own fee-structure announcement covering the change that took effect on 9 April 2026.

The discrepancy is a versioning problem. Gate has rewritten its spot and futures schedule more than once, and third-party trackers scrape whatever their last crawl found. Some still carry the older rate. The number that actually gets deducted is the one shown in your account while logged in, so check the fee page there rather than trusting any article, including this one.

## Futures are closer than spot

At VIP 0, Gate charges **0.02% maker / 0.05% taker** on perpetual futures, with USDT-margined and BTC-margined perpetuals plus delivery contracts, and leverage up to 125x on selected pairs.

Binance's USDT-margined futures sit at **0.02% maker / 0.04% taker** at the standard tier. Half a basis point cheaper on the taker side. On a $50,000 position that's $5 — real, but small enough that it should not be the deciding factor. Funding rates settle every eight hours on perpetuals, and over a multi-day hold they routinely cost more than the trading fee itself. Neither platform is "cheap" if you ignore funding.

### How Gate decides your tier

Two paths, and you get whichever one is better for you:

1. **30-day trading volume.** Spot (including convert) counts at full weight. Perpetual and delivery futures count at 40%. Options at 20%. CFDs at 10%. That weighting matters — $1 million of futures turnover is not $1 million of qualifying volume.
2. **GT holdings.** Measured as a 14-day average balance, so buying GT an hour before the snapshot won't work. Gate's own documentation puts VIP 1 at about 1,000 GT, VIP 5 at 20,000 GT, and VIP 9 at 200,000 GT.

The tier update runs automatically, and if you qualify through volume you get 60 days of downgrade protection after the upgrade. The GT path doubles as an investment decision, which is a different risk than a fee discount — GT's dollar value moves, so 20,000 GT is not a fixed dollar amount.

## Binance's ladder is shorter and steeper

Binance runs nine levels, VIP 0 through VIP 9. Third-party fee comparisons published in late 2025 put the entry point for VIP 1 at $1,000,000 in 30-day spot volume, or 25 BNB held. That's the tier that drops maker fees to 0.09%. VIP 3 sits around $20 million of volume or 250 BNB, at roughly 0.042% maker and 0.06% taker. From VIP 5 upward the maker fee collapses toward 0.012%.

Compare the first rungs: Gate's VIP 1 opens at $60,000 of 30-day volume. Binance's opens at $1,000,000. If you trade a few hundred thousand dollars a month, you're a mid-tier account on Gate and still a regular user on Binance.

That's the strongest argument for Gate in the entire fee discussion, and it has nothing to do with the headline rate.

One warning that applies to both platforms: don't manufacture volume to reach a tier. If you're $200,000 short of VIP 1, trading that extra $200,000 costs you roughly $200 in fees plus spread, while the discount on your normal volume saves a fraction of that. Chasing tiers is a losing trade for almost everyone who tries it. Accept the tier your real activity earns.

## Coin selection: thousands of tokens versus a few hundred

Public counts of Gate's catalog range from about 2,500 to 4,500 listed assets depending on the tracker and how it counts wrapped or duplicated assets. Binance's is commonly cited in the several-hundred range, with stricter vetting before listing. Whichever exact figure you use, the gap is large enough that it changes what's tradeable.

Practically, that means tokens available on Gate months before Binance lists them — and Binance's listing bar is partly a compliance filter, which is why some assets never arrive at all. Gate also runs Pilot Trading for early-stage assets and Startup for token sales, where allocations are tied to GT staking. Binance's equivalent is Launchpad, where subscriptions are tied to BNB holdings and competition for allocation is heavier.

The cost of that breadth is liquidity, and it's not a small cost. On a token listed on both venues, you'll generally see a deeper book and tighter spread on Binance. On a Gate-only micro cap, the book can be thin outside peak hours, and a $5,000 market order can move the price more than the fee ever would.

Before sizing up on any pair, look at the order book depth on the side you're about to hit. It takes ten seconds and it's worth more than any fee comparison.

## Where liquidity genuinely separates them

Gate is not small. It reported more than 17 million registered users and over $4.95 billion in daily volume as of 2026, which keeps it inside the top ten exchanges globally. That's a substantial book on major pairs.

Binance is bigger, and on cross-listed majors the difference shows up as slippage on large orders. The practical rule: majors, Binance has the edge; niche assets, you take Gate's book as it comes, because the alternative is not trading the asset at all.

## Security and transparency

Both exchanges publish proof-of-reserves data. Binance's system is built on a Merkle-tree structure users can check independently, and it also maintains SAFU, an emergency insurance fund financed from trading fees. Gate was an early adopter of PoR reporting and publishes its reserve data through its own channels.

Gate's account-level protections are thorough: multi-signature wallets for fund management, cold storage for the majority of user assets, two-factor authentication, an anti-phishing code, withdrawal whitelists, IP restrictions, and a separate fund password for sensitive operations.

The history is worth stating plainly rather than burying. Gate's predecessor operated as Bter.com and lost roughly 7,170 BTC in a 2015 breach. The platform rebranded, rebuilt its security architecture, and has not reported a major loss of customer funds since. A decade-old incident is context, not a current warning — but you should know it exists before trusting anyone's "perfect safety record" claim.

## Products beyond spot

Both platforms have moved well past spot trading. Gate covers USDT- and BTC-margined futures, options, copy trading, grid and DCA bots, staking and lending products, crypto loans, an NFT marketplace, and Pilot Trading. Binance covers futures, options, copy trading, trading bots, and its own Earn suite.

The differences that matter for cost are the native tokens. GT and BNB both reduce fees, both gate access to token sales, and both carry price risk if you're holding them purely for the discount.

## Getting money in and out

Crypto deposits are free on both. Withdrawal fees vary by asset and chain, and the chain you choose often matters more than the exchange — sending USDT over TRC-20 instead of ERC-20 is usually the single biggest withdrawal decision you'll make in a week. Fiat on-ramps exist on both but depend on third-party providers, and availability differs sharply by country. Check your region's options before assuming a card or bank transfer will work.

## Common questions

**Is Gate.io the same as Gate.com?**
Yes. The exchange rebranded its primary domain to Gate.com in 2025, and the Gate.io URL redirects there. Accounts, logins and services carried over.

**Is Gate cheaper than Binance?**
It depends on the market and your volume. Binance is cheaper for low-volume spot trading thanks to the BNB discount. Gate is cheaper on futures maker fees at the base tier and offers far easier entry into the fee ladder, with a top rate of 0% maker.

**Do either of them accept US users?**
No. Both global platforms block US residents. US traders need a domestically regulated exchange instead.

**Does holding the platform token always pay off?**
Not automatically. GT and BNB both reduce fees and unlock allocations, but you're taking on the price risk of the token. Run the numbers on your actual monthly volume before buying a token to chase a discount.

**Should I chase a higher VIP tier?**
Only if your existing volume already qualifies. Adding trades to reach a threshold almost always costs more in fees and spread than the discount returns.

## What this comes down to

Binance wins on depth, scale and the cheapest base-tier spot execution. Gate wins on breadth, earlier access to new assets, and a fee ladder that a mid-sized trader can actually climb — 17 tiers starting at $60,000 of monthly volume rather than $1 million.

Neither is the wrong choice. Picking the one that matches how you trade, rather than the one with the biggest name, is where the money is.
